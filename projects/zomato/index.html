Product case study · Zomato

Ordering experience & revenue

Improving the food-ordering experience for 20 million users — with revenue as the objective, which is a materially different brief from improving engagement and leads to a different shortlist.

FormatSelf-directed product case study, presented as a deck. Persona and funnel work, competitive teardown, and a prioritised intervention list.
SurfaceZomato — food delivery and restaurant discovery, India.
The questionWhere does the ordering funnel actually lose revenue, as opposed to where it loses attention?
FocusFunnel analysis, competitive positioning, monetisation.

Engagement and revenue are not the same funnel

In food delivery it is easy to improve the metrics that feel like progress — session length, items browsed, screens per visit — while leaving order value and order frequency untouched. Browsing is not a proxy for buying here; past a point it is a symptom of a user who cannot decide, and an undecided user is a user who orders from the same three places or does not order at all.

So the brief was set against revenue from the start, which changes what counts as a good idea: anything that adds a step to a decided user has to pay for itself.

How I worked it

  • Segmented by intent, not demographics — the decided user and the undecided user need opposite products, and the app serves them the same one.
  • Mapped the funnel to the money, so an intervention is judged by whether it moves order value or frequency.
  • Ran a competitive teardown against the nearest alternative to separate genuine gaps from cosmetic differences.
  • Prioritised by realistic impact, and wrote down what I deliberately was not proposing — usually the more interesting half of a shortlist.
