Increasing user growth

Value commerce in tier-2 and tier-3 India. The binding constraint is not selection or price — it is trust, data cost, and whether a first-time buyer believes the thing will actually arrive.

The constraint is trust, not price

It is tempting to treat value commerce as a pricing problem, because price is the visible difference. But a first-time online buyer in a tier-3 city is not comparing two prices — they are deciding whether to hand money to an app at all. Selection and discounting do not answer that question, and a growth plan built on them will buy installs that never convert.

Data cost matters for the same reason: every heavy screen between install and first order is a real, metered cost to the user, and it is spent before they have any evidence the product works.

How I worked it

  • Segmented by what the user is risking, not by what they are buying.
  • Traced the acquisition loop to the first completed order rather than to install, because that is where trust is actually created or lost.
  • Prioritised against the real constraint and dropped the interventions that only looked good against a selection-and-price framing.
  • Wrote the surviving intervention as a PRD, with the non-goals stated.
