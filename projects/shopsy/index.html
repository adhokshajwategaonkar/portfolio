Self-directed case study Growth · India Ships with a PRD
Growth strategy + PRD · Shopsy

Growing the next 200 million e-commerce users

Value commerce in tier-2 and tier-3 India. The binding constraint turned out to be trust and data cost rather than selection or price.

Read the full PRD
The problem

A first-time shopper in a tier-2 or tier-3 city abandons the order before paying.

Will the parcel arrive, what does the browsing cost in data, and has anyone they know done this before.

What I produced

Segmentation, behavioural analysis and experiments aimed at acquisition, trust and referral-driven growth.

The PRD is the deliverable, linked above and unedited.

The call

Tracing the acquisition loop to the first completed order rather than to install, because that is where trust gets created or lost.

Interventions that only worked under a price framing got dropped.

The constraint is trust, and price is the decoy

Treating value commerce as a pricing problem is tempting, because price is the visible difference between platforms. A first-time online buyer in a tier-3 city is not comparing two prices though. They are deciding whether to hand money to an app at all. Selection and discounting do not answer that, and a growth plan built on them buys installs that never convert.

Data cost compounds it. Every heavy screen between install and first order is a metered cost to the user, spent before they have any evidence the product works. So I segmented by what the user is risking rather than by what they are buying, then wrote the surviving intervention as a PRD with its non-goals stated.
